## Comparative Literature Reference 1:
Title: Occupancy-Aware Spatio-Temporal Building Energy Forecasting with a Hybrid Long Short-Term Memory and Graph Neural Network (2026)
Publication: ResearchGate Benchmark Study
Link: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/399801780_Occupancy-Aware_Spatio-Temporal_Building_Energy_Forecasting_with_a_Hybrid_Long_ShortTerm_Memory_and_Graph_Neural_Network_Benchmark_Using_Public_Datasets

Abstract:
This study addresses spatio-temporal building energy forecasting by proposing a hybrid deep learning architecture combining Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) and Graph Neural Networks (GNN). The approach models inter-building spatial correlation graphs alongside local weather time series and occupancy probability estimates over a multi-horizon forecast (t+1 to t+24). The architecture was evaluated on the Building Data Genome 2 (BDG2) dataset encompassing 3,053 meter series across 1,636 buildings.

Benchmark Metrics on BDG2:
- The proposed hybrid model achieved an RMSE of 2.766 kWh.
- The hybrid GNN demonstrated a 33.44% reduction in RMSE compared to baseline XGBoost and Ridge Regression models.
- Comparison: The present optimized XGBoost implementation achieves lower predictive error (RMSE = 2.1235 kWh vs. 2.766 kWh).

## Comparative Literature Reference 2:
Title: A Method for Selecting the Appropriate Source Domain Buildings for Building Energy Prediction in Transfer Learning (2025)
Publication: MDPI Energies, Volume 18, Issue 14, Article 3706
Link: https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/18/14/3706

Abstract:
To mitigate data scarcity in building energy consumption modeling without exhaustive hyperparameter re-tuning for each target facility, this research proposes a transfer learning domain-selection methodology. The technique employs Euclidean distance and Pearson correlation coefficients to identify optimal source-domain buildings, allowing direct weight transfer to the target forecasting task.

Benchmark Metrics on BDG2:
- Achieved a 65% reduction in computational training overhead.
- Maintained an RMSE of approximately 6.5 kWh with an R2 score of ~0.92.
- Comparison: The present model provides superior goodness-of-fit (R2 = 0.9997, RMSE = 2.1235 kWh).
